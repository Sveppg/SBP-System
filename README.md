## Project Overview

SBP-System is an embedded condition-monitoring and predictive-maintenance research project developed as part of a Bachelor's thesis in Technical Computer Science, specializing in Embedded Systems.

The objective is to investigate whether mechanical abnormalities, particularly changes in valve clearance, can be detected through acoustic and vibration analysis.

The system is developed and evaluated using a Ducati Monster S2R 800, equipped with an 803 cc, 90° V-twin, four-stroke engine with desmodromic valve actuation.

The long-term vision is a portable diagnostic tool that assists mechanics in assessing engine condition and identifying potential maintenance requirements.

## Hardware

### Sensors and Actuators

- **SEN-VIB01:** Piezoelectric vibration sensor
- **SPH0645:** Digital I²S MEMS microphone (part number to be verified)
- **VMA438:** 128 × 64 OLED display
- **ESP32-S3-N16R8:** Development board with 16 MB Flash and 8 MB PSRAM

The hardware configuration may change depending on the measurement quality and experimental evaluation of the prototype.

## Research Objectives

The primary research objective is to evaluate whether acoustic and mechanical vibration measurements can be used to detect changes in valve clearance.

The investigation focuses on:

1. Acquisition of acoustic and mechanical vibration signals using an ESP32-S3.
2. Digital signal processing using RMS, FFT and transient-event analysis.
3. Extraction of characteristic features associated with mechanical valve-train events.
4. Comparison of recordings obtained under different, documented valve-clearance conditions.
5. Evaluation of whether combining acoustic and vibration features improves anomaly detection.
6. Assessment of real-time performance and resource utilization on the embedded system.

## Audio Acquisition

The SPH0645 is a mono I²S microphone. Its `SEL` pin is connected to GND, selecting the left I²S channel.

The initial acquisition configuration uses a sampling frequency of **32 kHz**.

According to the Nyquist–Shannon sampling theorem, the theoretical upper frequency limit is 16 kHz. The actual usable bandwidth depends on the microphone's frequency response, internal filtering and signal quality.

At an engine speed of 8,000 RPM:

- One crankshaft revolution takes 7.5 ms.
- One complete four-stroke cycle (720°) takes 15 ms.
- At 32 kHz, 240 samples are captured per crankshaft revolution.
- Therefore, 480 samples are captured per complete four-stroke cycle.

This temporal resolution provides a basis for investigating recurring acoustic events associated with valve-train operation.

## Vibration Acquisition

The SEN-VIB01 is used to investigate mechanical vibrations and transient impulses generated by engine operation.

Its suitability for detecting small valve-clearance deviations will be evaluated experimentally.

Continuous sampling, signal conditioning and defined sensor mounting are important requirements for reliable vibration analysis.

## Signal Processing

The planned signal-processing pipeline consists of:

**Data Acquisition → Filtering → Feature Extraction → Anomaly Detection → Diagnostic Output**

Possible features include RMS, peak amplitude, frequency-domain energy, spectral characteristics and transient impulse patterns.

The initial goal is to detect statistically significant differences between documented engine conditions, rather than to estimate exact valve-clearance values.

## Experimental Validation

Measurements will be performed using the same acquisition system under controlled and reproducible conditions.

The experimental dataset will include recordings obtained before and after a professionally performed valve-clearance adjustment.

Measurement conditions such as engine speed, temperature, sensor placement and mounting method will be documented.

The evaluation will examine detection performance, measurement repeatability, signal quality, execution time and memory usage.

## Project Scope

This project is initially intended as a proof-of-concept embedded diagnostic system.

A successful prototype may serve as the foundation for a future workshop diagnostic tool. However, reliable commercial diagnosis would require further validation across multiple engines and operating conditions.

The system is not intended to replace prescribed mechanical valve-clearance inspections during the research phase.
